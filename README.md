# Supercharged: Earth's Rising Heat & Cyclonic Storms

An interactive web report and educational laboratory visualization investigating the empirical relationship between 25 years of global atmospheric warming and tropical cyclone intensity, accumulated energy, and coastal impacts (1999–2024).

---

## Core Scientific Finding

A pervasive public misconception posits that higher global temperatures generate hundreds of additional tropical storms annually. Comprehensive observational records from NOAA, NASA GISTEMP, and IBTrACS disprove this assumption:
- **Global storm frequency remains stable** at ~85 to 88 systems per year due to atmospheric stabilization and mid-troposphere warming.
- **Storm intensity is supercharging**: Elevated Sea Surface Temperatures (SST > 26.5°C) and record Upper Ocean Heat Content (OHC) inject massive thermal energy directly into cyclonic cores.
- The proportion of catastrophic **Category 4 and 5 hurricanes has risen from ~22.8% (1999–2003) to ~35.4% (2019–2024)**.

## Visualizations Included

1. **Earth's Rising Fever (1999–2024):** Line chart tracking global mean surface temperature anomalies relative to the 20th-century baseline (+0.45°C in 1999 to +1.28°C in 2024).
2. **The Deceptive Flatline (Myth-Buster):** Dual-axis comparative plot contrasting rising global temperatures against steady annual storm frequency (~85.2 storms/yr).
3. **The Monster Shift:** 100% stacked bar chart illustrating 5-year climatological shifts in Saffir-Simpson storm intensity proportions (Cat 1–2 vs. Cat 3 vs. Cat 4–5).
4. **The Power Surge:** Dual-axis chart tracking Accumulated Cyclone Energy (ACE Index) alongside Upper Ocean Heat Content (0–700 m depth anomaly).
5. **Geographic Genesis & Intensification Zones:** Interactive Leaflet GIS map with quick-focus basin controls (Gulf/Atlantic, Western Pacific Typhoon Alley, Acapulco/Otis, Central US Convective Corridor) and SST thermal warm pools.
6. **The Ground Impact:** Clausius-Clapeyron atmospheric physics breakdown (+7% atmospheric moisture holding capacity per 1°C warming) paired with coastal surge evidence.
